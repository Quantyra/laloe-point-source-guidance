## What the statements must quantify over

Take \(N\ge1\), \(D=3N\), Euclidean configuration space with its Lebesgue measure, \(G,m,\hbar,\epsilon,c>0\), and \(t_1<t_2\). Use the closed interval \(I=[t_1,t_2]\). Bind \(Q,\rho,j,W\) to the **same** history. Preserve the paper's \(C^1\) center on \(I\), the subluminal bound on each particle over the necessary past, the retained diagonal term, and the finite time integral of \(\langle W\rangle=\int W\rho\). Bind the local density bounds \(0<\rho_{\min}\le\rho\le\rho_{\max}\), joint continuity, local integrability of current and source, and the measurable spatial essential supremum in (5). One may flatten the coordinates into Euclidean \(D\)-space after proving the coordinate-projection bound and matching the measure. A supremum norm on particle tuples changes the ball and its constants.

The first theorem excludes such data satisfying (3) and (5), including a continuous current agreeing with \(\rho\dot Q\) on the path. The proposition **replaces** (5) with a uniformly spatially Lipschitz density, a measurable local phase with spatial \(W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}\) regularity and the current representation, its first-order expansion at the center with \(\dot Q=(\hbar/m)b\), and the integrable-in-time majorant for the scaled remainder. It must have a proof without deriving a supremum bound on the phase gradient.
