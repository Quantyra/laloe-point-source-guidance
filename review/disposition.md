## Mathematical and editorial decisions

- **Moving cutoff and sign.** The manuscript derives the relative current in a moving tube. Endpoint masses scale as the tube volume. A positive self term contributes at least surface order over a fixed time interval. The inner-ball bound now states the coordinate inequality explicitly. We keep a lower bound with half the available geometric constant.
- **Thin shells and the phase gradient.** Grok noted in its blind pass that small oscillations of phase can produce large gradients on individual spheres. The proof uses a smooth radial cutoff and integrates the current by parts against the density times the cutoff gradient. The resulting bound involves the phase remainder itself. Spatial Lipschitz control of the density and an integrable temporal majorant give the needed little-o estimate. We make no necessity claim for that majorant. Rapid oscillations on a single sphere do not refute the proposition.
- **Regularity.** The first theorem now uses a time-integrated spatial essential supremum, with continuity as a sufficient case. The text specifies a continuously differentiable center on the test interval, a measurable phase expansion and weak spatial gradients for the second assertion. For the frozen Coulomb example the density is Lipschitz, with a radial cusp at the origin. The word “smooth” in Claude’s first manuscript report is inaccurate there. The phase is also Lipschitz, but lacks a derivative at the origin. Lipschitz regularity by itself is insufficient for the phase proposition.
- **Which equation?** The revised title names the all-pairs reading. The article distinguishes the displayed density product, the author's wording about other particles, and the positive-width alternative. AGY’s suggestion that a finite width is strictly necessary is stronger than the result. Dropping self terms in a collision-free tube removes this local obstruction, and alternative definitions of a singular velocity have not been excluded. The ordinary Bohmian global existence theorem does not establish global avoidance for a different, nonlinear off-diagonal law.
- **Observable significance.** The two-port protocol and its 55% ordinary benchmark remain in the README and diagnostic. The revised manuscript ends with the consequence for obtaining a pointer probability. There is no estimate of an actual collapse-model success rate.
- **Last editorial pass.** The text specifies measurability of the essential supremum, calls it a hypothesis, and corrects sentence capitalization. It also displays the bound on the phase remainder and ties the frozen example to each fixed positive coupling, as Grok suggested. These editorial changes followed the checks on the linked draft snapshots. The build check is separate from the analytical proof.
